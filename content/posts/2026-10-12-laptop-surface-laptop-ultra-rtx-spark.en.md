Microsoft's Surface Laptop Ultra, open for preorder since October 7, is built on Nvidia's RTX Spark (N1X). The headline is the memory ladder: the chip tier matters less than how much unified memory you buy with it.

## Two chip tiers, a wide price gap

| Spec | Base | Higher tier |
|---|---|---|
| CPU | 18 cores | 20 cores |
| GPU (CUDA cores) | 5,120 | 6,144 |
| Memory / storage | 24GB / 512GB | from 32GB / 1TB |
| US price | $2,599 | — (top 128GB/1TB config: $5,899.99) |
| Germany | €2,899 | full spec €4,239 |
| Display | 15" Mini-LED, up to 2,000 nits | same |
| Preorder / ships | Oct 7 / Oct 16 | same |

## Memory is the real price tag

The 128GB configuration costs $5,899.99, more than double the base. CPU and GPU share one pool of memory, so anyone running large models locally will buy up for capacity, not for the extra two cores. Reports put AI throughput at up to one petaflop (FP4).

Nvidia has not published an official memory-bandwidth figure. Some outlets cite roughly 300GB/s, and the laptop chip's power range is reported at 45–80W. How long a thin Surface chassis can sustain that is a question for independent reviews.

## The competition is just arriving

Other laptops use the same chip. According to Notebookcheck, Asus's ProArt P14 and P16 get their first US units on October 16, and Dell's XPS 16 Creator Edition pairs it with a 16-inch 3.2K Tandem OLED. We could not confirm their prices, so the fair comparison waits for October 16, when like-for-like configurations have price tags.

Sources: [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/New-16-inch-laptop-confirmed-to-launch-in-October-with-128-GB-RAM-and-20-core-Nvidia-RTX-Spark.1412201.0.html)

In short: the 24GB base is $2,599 for early access to the new chip, and only the high-memory tiers make sense for local AI. Real-world performance and thermals have to wait for shipping units.
